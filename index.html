TigerX Privacy Policy

Last updated: 24 November 2026

This Privacy Policy explains how the TigerX mobile app (“the app”), provided by the TigerX team (“we”, “us”, “our”), uses information. We wrote it in simple words, so that you can see exactly what happens with your information. By using the app, you accept this Privacy Policy. If you do not accept it, please do not use the app.

1. Summary

  • TigerX has no accounts. You never give us your name, email address or phone number.
  • We do not sell your information. The app has no ads and no analytics or tracking tools.
  • Your test history and settings stay on your phone.
  • To run a speed test, the app connects to third-party LibreSpeed test servers, which can see your IP address.
  • The app sends one anonymous “+1” to count installs. It contains no personal information.

2. Information stored on your device

When you run a test, TigerX saves the result in the History screen on your phone. A saved result contains:

  • download speed, upload speed, ping, jitter and the amount of data used by the test;
  • the date and time of the test and the name of the test server;
  • the connection type (WiFi, mobile data or ethernet);
  • the name of your WiFi network (only if you allowed the location permission, see section 4);
  • the local IP address of your phone inside your network and your public IP address;
  • the name of your internet provider and your approximate city and country, which are worked out from your IP address (this is not your GPS location).

The app also saves your settings (theme, speed unit and the test server you chose) and a note that your install has already been counted. All of this stays on your device. We do not receive it.

3. Information sent over the internet

TigerX needs the internet to work. These are the only cases where it sends or receives data:

  • Server list: when the app opens, it downloads the list of public LibreSpeed test servers from librespeed.org. That website can see your IP address and standard connection information, like any website you visit.
  • Speed tests: the app sends and receives random test data and ping requests to the test server you selected. The person who runs that server can see your IP address and the time of the test. The server also tells the app your public IP address, your internet provider and your approximate location (city and country) so the app can show them to you. The server may use an IP lookup service for this.
  • Install counter: the first time the app opens, it sends one request that adds “+1” to a counter in our database, which is hosted by Google Firebase (Realtime Database). The app then reads the total number and shows it on the home screen. This request contains no name, email address, device ID or advertising ID. Like any internet request, it lets Google’s servers see your IP address, but we do not use it to identify you. After the first time, the app only reads the number.

The app does not send your test history, WiFi name or local IP address to us or to anyone else.

4. Location permission and WiFi name

On Android, an app cannot read the name of the WiFi network you are connected to unless it has location permission. TigerX asks for this permission only to show your WiFi name on the screen and in your own History.

We do not read your GPS position. We do not store or send your location, and we do not track you. The “approximate location” in the app comes from your IP address and is not related to this permission.

The permission is optional. If you refuse it, everything works except that the WiFi name is not shown. You can change it at any time in your phone’s Settings → Apps → TigerX → Permissions.

5. Information we do not collect

TigerX does not collect:

  • your name, email address, phone number or home address;
  • your contacts, photos, files, messages, camera or microphone data;
  • your GPS or precise location;
  • advertising IDs or other device identifiers;
  • payment information.

The app has no user accounts, no ads, no analytics tools and no crash-reporting tools.

6. How we use information

We use the information described above only to:

  • run speed tests and show you the results;
  • show details about your connection (type, provider, IP address, approximate location);
  • keep your history and settings on your phone;
  • pick a test server for you;
  • count installs and show that number in the app;
  • prevent misuse of the app and our systems, and follow the law.

If you are in the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom, our reasons for this are: providing the service you asked for (running speed tests) and our legitimate interest in counting installs.

7. Third-party services

We use these third-party services. They have their own privacy practices, which we do not control:

  • LibreSpeed public test servers and server list (librespeed.org): run by independent people and organisations around the world.
  • Google Firebase Realtime Database: stores the install counter number. Google’s privacy policy is available at policies.google.com/privacy.
  • Google Play: if you got the app from Google Play, Google gives us general statistics, such as the number of installs and crashes. We do not receive personal information about you from this.

8. Sharing and selling information

We do not sell, rent or trade your personal information. We do not share it with anyone, except: the technical data that the test servers and Firebase need to do their job (see section 3), and when the law requires us to.

9. Data retention and deletion

Your history and settings stay on your phone until you delete them. You can delete them in any of these ways:

  • in TigerX: Settings → Data → Clear, or the bin icon on the History screen (swipe a single result to delete only that one);
  • in Android: Settings → Apps → TigerX → Storage → Clear data;
  • by uninstalling the app, which removes everything the app saved on your phone.

The install counter is only a number. It contains no personal information, so there is nothing about you to delete from it. Test servers and Google may keep technical logs for their own reasons, under their own policies.

10. Security

We use encrypted (HTTPS) connections wherever they are available. Some test servers may only offer an unencrypted connection. In that case the traffic of that test (random test data and your IP address) is not encrypted. No method of sending data over the internet is completely secure, so we cannot promise absolute security.

11. Children’s privacy

TigerX is not made for children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children. The app does not ask for personal information at all. If you believe that a child has given us personal information, please contact us and we will delete it.

12. Your choices and rights

You are in control: you can refuse or withdraw the location permission, delete your history and settings, or uninstall the app at any time.

Depending on where you live (for example in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or California), you may have the right to ask us for access to, correction or deletion of personal information we hold about you, to object to or limit how it is used, and to complain to your local data protection authority. Because we do not keep personal information that identifies you, we usually have nothing to give or delete, but you are welcome to contact us and we will answer your request.

13. International data transfers

Our install counter database is hosted by Google in Singapore, and LibreSpeed test servers are in many different countries. When you use the app, your IP address and test traffic may be processed in countries other than your own, where data protection laws can be different.

14. Changes to this policy

We may update this Privacy Policy, for example when the app changes. We will change the date at the top of this page when we do. If you keep using the app after a change, you accept the updated policy.
